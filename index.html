The record, moving

In Their Own Footage

Nine moments, laid out in order, from five verified archives — British Pathé, Prasar Bharati's official record, the Huntley Film Archives, CRUX, and the BBC.

13 April 1919

Jallianwala Bagh

No film camera recorded the massacre itself — this documentary reconstructs, from survivor testimony and the historical record, the events in the walled garden in Amritsar that hardened a nation against colonial rule.

Source: CRUX documentary explainer
12 March – 6 April 1930

The Salt March

Gandhi and a small band of followers walk 240 miles from Sabarmati to the coastal village of Dandi to make salt from the sea in open defiance of the British salt tax — a simple act that becomes the spark for nationwide civil disobedience.

Source: Huntley Film Archives, contemporary newsreel
September 1931

Gandhi Goes to London

Newsreel cameras meet Gandhi off the boat in England, there for the Second Round Table Conference — Congress's sole representative, in his own dhoti, before the British establishment. The talks collapse without an agreement on self-rule.

Source: British Pathé, contemporary newsreel
August 1942

The Trouble in India

British newsreel coverage of the unrest following the Quit India call — reported, tellingly, from the empire's own point of view, as disorder to be contained rather than a nation demanding its freedom.

Source: British Pathé, contemporary newsreel
1943 – 1945

Netaji's Army

Subhas Chandra Bose slips out of British house arrest, travels by submarine to Southeast Asia, and raises the Indian National Army to fight for freedom from outside British India's borders entirely.

Source: Prasar Bharati Archives, “Azadi Ki Kahani”
March 1947

The Last Viceroy

Lord Mountbatten arrives in Delhi as Britain's final Viceroy, with instructions to transfer power within months rather than years — the moment the empire's timeline compresses from decades to weeks.

Source: British Pathé, contemporary newsreel
August 1947

A Million on the Move

Newsreel footage of the largest mass migration in history, as Partition redraws the map and some fifteen million people cross a new border in both directions, on foot, by cart, and by train.

Source: British Pathé, contemporary newsreel
14 August 1947

Tryst with Destiny

On the eve of independence, Jawaharlal Nehru addresses the Constituent Assembly with the speech that would define the moment for generations: a pledge redeemed after generations of struggle.

Source: Prasar Bharati Archives, official record
15 August 1947

The Night India Became Free

As the clock strikes midnight, crowds gather outside the Constituent Assembly in Delhi. Freedom arrives inseparably twinned with Partition — a joy shadowed by displacement and loss on a vast scale.

Source: BBC News India, From The Archives
